## Course Scope & Objectives
Network Theory bridges foundational physics/calculus and upper-division ECE subjects such as Analog Circuits, Control Systems, Signals & Systems, and RF Engineering. It establishes systematic methods for analyzing lumped linear time-invariant (LTI) electrical networks in the time domain, phasor ($j\omega$) domain, and complex frequency ($s$) domain.

## Learning Outcomes
1. Formulate nodal, mesh, cut-set, and tie-set equations for arbitrary lumped networks using graph theory.
2. Simplify linear networks using Thevenin, Norton, Superposition, Reciprocity, Tellegen, and Maximum Power Transfer theorems.
3. Solve first- and second-order $RL$, $RC$, and $RLC$ transient problems using differential equations and Laplace transforms with initial conditions.
4. Analyze series/parallel resonance, quality factor ($Q$), bandwidth ($BW$), and magnetically coupled circuits.
5. Characterize and interconnect two-port networks via $Z$, $Y$, $h$, $g$, and $ABCD$ parameters.
6. Test positive realness and synthesize lossless $LC$ and $RC$ one-port driving-point functions using Foster and Cauer forms.
